# Project Overview

## What this is

The **qijk backend** is the API behind the [qijk widget](https://github.com/JessengBijleng/Qijk-widget). The widget lets visitors of a customer's website create visualizations of **kitchen wraps**: see what their own kitchen would look like after its cabinets and surfaces are wrapped in a different foil (color, pattern or finish).

The widget is a frontend that customers embed on their websites in an **iframe**. Everything it can't do in the browser happens here: generating the visualization with an AI, checking that the widget is allowed to be used, and keeping track of what visitors did.

Read the widget's [docs/overview.md](https://github.com/JessengBijleng/Qijk-widget/blob/main/docs/overview.md) too. Both projects share the roles and glossary below.

## Who is involved

| Role | Who they are | What they want |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer** | A business (e.g. a kitchen wrapping company) that embeds the widget on its website. | Visitors who are convinced and turn into leads or sales. |
| **Visitor / end user** | Someone browsing the customer's website, often a potential client of the customer. | To see what their kitchen could look like before they commit. |
| **Widget** | The frontend in the iframe. This API's only client for now. | Fast, reliable answers it can show to the visitor. |
| **AI provider** | The external service that generates the visualizations. | Not a person; a paid dependency we call on the visitor's behalf. |

## Responsibilities

This repository is responsible for:

- **Generating visualizations.** Receiving the visitor's kitchen photo and chosen wrap, calling the AI that renders the wrapped kitchen, and returning the result to the widget.
- **Authorizing widgets.** Deciding whether a widget request comes from a known customer and an allowed website, and refusing it otherwise. Every AI call costs money, so nobody should be able to use the API for free.
- **Storing visitor requests.** Recording what visitors did (photos uploaded, wraps chosen, visualizations generated) in the database, scoped to the customer they belong to.
- **Being the source of truth for the photo.** Where the visitor's photo is processed, who it is shared with and how long it is kept is decided and enforced here. The widget's privacy copy must match what this backend actually does.

This repository is **not** responsible for:

- The visitor's experience. All UI, copy, translations and client-side validation live in the widget.
- The customer's own website.
- Rendering the visualization itself. The AI provider does that; we prepare the input, call it and store the output.

## Constraints from serving an embedded widget

Every change to this project has to respect these constraints:

- **Cross-origin.** The widget runs inside iframes on customers' domains. The API must answer CORS requests, and only for origins that belong to a known customer.
- **No cookies.** Browsers often block or partition third-party cookies in iframes. Don't rely on sessions or cookies for widget requests; authorize with something the widget sends explicitly.
- **Nothing in the widget is secret.** Anything the widget sends (keys, IDs, config) can be read and copied by anyone visiting the host page. Authorization can't depend on a value in the widget alone.
- **Multiple customers.** One backend serves many customers. Every record belongs to a customer, every query is scoped to one, and anything customer-specific (allowed origins, upload limits, wraps, branding) is configuration, not code.
- **Cost and abuse.** AI calls are slow and paid. Rate-limit per customer and per visitor, and never call the AI before a request has been authorized and validated.
- **Validate everything again.** The widget validates uploads (JPG, PNG, WEBP and HEIC, up to 15 MB by default) for a good experience, but the API must enforce the same rules itself.
- **Personal data.** A photo of someone's kitchen is personal data. Store as little as needed, for as short as needed, and never log photos or their contents.

## Glossary

- **Wrap / kitchen wrap**: a foil or film applied over existing kitchen cabinets, doors and surfaces to change how they look without replacing them.
- **Visualization**: the image the visitor creates that shows their kitchen with one or more wraps applied. Generated by the AI provider through this API.
- **Embed**: the snippet (iframe) a customer places on their website to load the widget.
- **Request**: something a visitor did through the widget that we store, such as uploading a photo or generating a visualization.

## Decisions

- **Tech stack:** Laravel with Pest for tests and Pint for formatting. See [development.md](development.md).
- **The widget is the only client** for now. There is no customer-facing dashboard yet.

## Open questions

These aren't decided yet. Don't make assumptions about them; ask, or record the decision here once it's made:

- Hosting.
- Which AI provider and model generate the visualizations, and whether a request waits for the result or the widget polls for it.
- How a widget authenticates: what identifies a customer, how allowed origins are checked, and whether visitors get a short-lived token.
- **What happens to the visitor's photo** (purpose, retention, where it's processed, who it's shared with). The widget's privacy copy is a placeholder until this is decided.
- What exactly is stored per request, and for how long.
- How a customer's widget config (locale, upload rules, example images, wraps) is managed and served to the widget.
- Where the wrap catalog comes from and who manages it.
- What happens with a finished visualization (download, share, send to the customer as a lead).
- Whether the backend hands a session over from desktop to phone (the widget's QR code idea).
